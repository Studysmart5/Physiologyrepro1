The essentials

Not the lecture — just the rules, definitions, and numbers worth knowing cold.

Core definitions

  • Reproduction: sperm + ovum develop, grow, unite → unique offspring.
  • Zygote → embryo → fetus, inside the uterus.
  • Gonads: testes (male) / ovaries (female) — produce gametes.

Sex determination

  • Fixed at fertilization, by the sex chromosomes.
  • Male = XY, Female = XX. Y is the smaller chromosome.
  • Y present → testes. Y absent → ovaries.
  • Male genitalia need a functional, secreting testis; default (no testis) = female.

Gonad differentiation

  • Both gonads start as the urogenital ridge, undifferentiated to week 6.
  • SRY gene (on Y) triggers testis formation in week 7.
  • No SRY → ovary forms in the same site.

Duct system rule

  • Two ducts present in every fetus: Wolffian & Müllerian.
  • Male: Wolffian persists, Müllerian regresses.
  • Female: Müllerian persists, Wolffian regresses.
  • External genitalia come from separate tissue, not either duct.

Hormonal switches

  • Fetal testis secretes testosterone + AMH (Sertoli/Leydig cells).
  • AMH → Müllerian ducts regress.
  • Testosterone → Wolffian ducts mature.
  • DHT (from testosterone) → penis + scrotum.

Hormones, general

  • Gonads: gametogenesis + sex hormone secretion.
  • Androgens = masculinizing; estrogens = feminizing — both in both sexes.
  • FSH + LH (pituitary) drive gonads; sex hormones + inhibin B feed back to suppress them.
  • Gonadotropins: noncyclic in men, cyclic in postpubertal women.

Puberty milestones

  • Girls: 8–13 yrs · Boys: 9–14 yrs.
  • Girls in order: thelarche (breasts) → pubarche (hair) → menarche (first period).
  • First cycles are usually anovulatory; ovulation regularizes ~1 yr later.
  • Adrenarche (adrenal androgen rise): girls 8–10, boys 10–12.

Menopause

  • Cause: ovaries stop responding to gonadotropins as primordial follicles decline.
  • Perimenopause: 45–55 yrs, up to 10 yrs, FSH rises before LH.
  • Average onset: 52 yrs.
  • Hot flushes in ~75% of women, may persist up to 40 yrs.
  • "Male menopause" (andropause) is not clearly established.

High yield

The numbers, exceptions, and classic exam traps most likely to be tested.

Numbers to memorize

  • Gonads undifferentiated until wk 6; SRY acts wk 7.
  • Duct differentiation: male 7–8 wk, female 8–9 wk.
  • Puberty: girls 8–13 yr, boys 9–14 yr.
  • Adrenarche: girls 8–10 yr, boys 10–12 yr.
  • DHEA peaks ~age 25 in women, slightly later in men.
  • Perimenopause 45–55 yr (up to 10 yr); average menopause onset 52 yr.
  • Hot flushes in ~75% of menopausal women, up to 40 yr.

"Default pathway" logic

  • No SRY → ovary forms (default), not "nothing."
  • No AMH → Müllerian ducts persist (default).
  • No testosterone → Wolffian ducts regress (default).
  • No testosterone/DHT → external genitalia are female (default).
  • Male development is the exception that needs active signals (SRY → AMH + testosterone); female is what happens if nothing acts.

Who secretes what (fetal testis)

  • Sertoli cells → AMH (kills Müllerian ducts).
  • Leydig cells → testosterone (saves Wolffian ducts; source of DHT).
  • DHT (from testosterone, in target tissue) → penis, scrotum, prostate — not the Wolffian derivatives themselves.

Feedback axis, both sexes

  • GnRH → FSH + LH → gonadal hormones + inhibin B.
  • Inhibin B selectively suppresses FSH only — a common distractor is "suppresses both FSH and LH."
  • Male gonadotropin secretion: noncyclic. Postpubertal female: cyclic/sequential (needed for menstruation, pregnancy, lactation).

Testosterone across life

  • Two prepubertal bursts: fetal (drives genital differentiation) and neonatal (function unknown).
  • Leydig cells then go quiescent — near-zero levels through childhood.
  • Pubertal rise → adult plateau (highest sustained level) → slow senescent decline.

Menopause mechanism

  • Root cause: decline in primordial follicles, not "hormone deficiency" per se.
  • FSH rises before LH in perimenopause (inhibin B falls first, disinhibiting FSH specifically).
  • Residual estrogen after menopause = peripheral aromatization of androstenedione, not ovarian secretion.
  • "Andropause" in men is not clearly established — a favorite true/false trap.

Classic mix-up pairs

  • Sex determination (genetic, fixed at fertilization) vs. sex differentiation (the fetal developmental process that follows).
  • Thelarche (breast development) → pubarche (axillary/pubic hair) → menarche (first period) — don't reorder them.
  • Puberty loosely = adolescence in casual use, but strictly defined it's the point reproduction becomes possible.
  • AMH acts on ducts (Müllerian regression); testosterone/DHT act on ducts + external genitalia — don't swap their jobs.
  • Menarche ≠ fertility: initial cycles are anovulatory; regular ovulation follows about a year later.
